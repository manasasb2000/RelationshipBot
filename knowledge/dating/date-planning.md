# Plan around the people

A good date fits both people’s energy, access needs, budget, location, and comfort. Ask whether they want playful, quiet, creative, outdoors, food-centered, or exploratory time. Pair one main activity with unstructured time to talk, and keep a weather or energy-level backup.

# Build connection

Shared novelty can make a date memorable, while familiar rituals can create safety. Examples include a neighborhood photo walk, cooking one unfamiliar dish together, a bookstore exchange, a small workshop, a picnic with conversation cards, a volunteer shift, or recreating an early shared memory. Current events and venue details should be checked live rather than assumed.
